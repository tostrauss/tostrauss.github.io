Four things I insist on

Every one of these came out of something that broke, or nearly did.

01

Put the guarantee where it can't be bypassed

Gastropoly's tenant isolation lives in Postgres Row Level Security, not in a controller. A forgotten filter returns nothing instead of another restaurant's payroll. If a rule matters, it belongs below the layer where people make mistakes.

02

The unglamorous half is the product

Moderation, reporting, rate limiting, GDPR export and deletion, backups that have actually been restored. None of it demos well, all of it decides whether a product survives its first real week.

03

Design for the worst environment, not the demo

The venue's Wi-Fi drops, the phone is old, the user is a seasonal worker on their first shift. Offline-first with a sync queue and an interface that needs no training is not polish — it's the requirement.

04

Small team, strict process

One slice per branch, mandatory review, coordinated migration numbering across three engineers, 370+ automated test files gating every merge. Process is what lets a small team move fast twice.
